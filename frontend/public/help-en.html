1. Connect devices

“Connect devices” opens the ioBroker object tree. Expand adapters, instances and channels, or search by object ID and name. The role and room filters narrow the selection. Mark datapoints and connect them with “Apply”. Devices that are already connected are locked against being selected twice. A light made of several datapoints (switch, dimmer, colour, white tone) appears there as one entry 💡, a TV or speaker as one entry 🎵 with volume, title, app and input.

2. Edit the floor plan

In the editor, create floors, rooms, doors and furniture. Assign the rooms to the ioBroker rooms. With the selection symbol ▤, pick a datapoint in the object tree: the connected ones that match the kind of device come first, all the others below; one not connected yet is connected when chosen. “↻ Refresh” reads the datapoints again.

Device type and parts: At a device in the editor, below its datapoint, is its “Device type” (light, switch, blind/shutter/awning, heating/air conditioning, fan, lock, media, sensor, binary sensor) and below it the “Datapoints of the device”: each part with its datapoint, “Choose …” and × to remove it; rare parts are under “More parts”. Another type looks for its parts beside the datapoint by itself (by role and name); what it does not find you choose with “Choose …” in the object browser. Example LED strip: type “Light”, with switch, brightness, colour (#RRGGBB, red/green/blue or hue/saturation) and colour temperature (Kelvin, mired or 0–100 %). The 3D view offers what the device has: no colour, no colour picker; no colour temperature, no white tone.

Template (floor plan image): “Scale with a ruler” scales the picture by a stretch of known length. If a scan is stretched in one direction, tick “Only in this direction”: then only the picture’s width or only its depth changes, whichever side the stretch follows – so measure one stretch across, then one along. “Depth stretched (%)” shows the ratio and can be entered directly too.

3. View and control

In the 3D view, open a room and operate its devices. Values arrive immediately on every change over the connection of the web adapter. Complex devices may need additional mappings for brightness, temperature or position; these are under “Advanced mappings”.

Glass cards: Under “Show” choose how the glass cards over the devices appear: “Cards always”, “Cards on hover” (the device shows only a glowing dot; the card appears while the mouse is on the dot or the card, a click or tap on the dot keeps it open, a second one closes it) or “No cards”. The choice is kept in this browser; without one the cards appear on hover.

TV: YouTube, Netflix, HDMI: A long press on a TV or speaker (with the mouse: hold it down) opens its quick menu with playback, volume and, under “Play”, a chip for each app and each input. A tap writes the value to the datapoint of the app (role media.app) or of the input (media.input); the card shows the app running. The chips come from the datapoint’s list of values (common.states). If the device lacks the datapoint, choose “App (YouTube …)” or “Input (HDMI …)” under “More parts” in the editor.

Boats

Under ⚙ → House: “Heading from a compass” turns the plan with the boat, “Position” takes the GPS for the position of the sun, “Sea around the boat” puts water at the waterline, with waves from a wave height and a wave length sensor. The furniture piece “Anchor” (Outside) belongs at the front on the bow roller; unturned, its chain runs to the top of the floor plan. With the chain counter and the water depth it sits in the roller (below 0.3 m of chain), hangs on the chain or lies on the bottom. The distance to the anchor (GPS) tightens the chain, and the alarm radius appears as a circle on the water. With the sensor “Speed” (log or SOG) the water flows past the hull, with a bow wave and a wake.

In the editor under Furniture → “Boat”: Tank (choose the contents, the level in % and optionally the litres; yellow when a supply runs low or a black water tank gets full), Rudder (on the rudder stock, with the rudder angle in °, positive = starboard), Drive with propeller (under the engine, with the rpm) and Navigation light (anchor light, steaming light, port, starboard, bicolour light, stern light; with its switch; height above the floor, e.g. 18 m for the masthead, and the mast as well if you like). Rudder and propeller hang below the floor they stand on. A click on a tank, rudder, drive, hatch or navigation light opens the details of its sensor with the history, when a history adapter records it. The gas bottle (search “gas”, also for a house, a grill or camping) is such a tank holding gas: a bottle with its valve and protective collar, the liquid gas inside up to its level. Beside the level it can show the contents; coming from a scale in kg, they are shown in kg.

The Hatch (square with rounded corners, or round) belongs on the deck. With a contact (open/closed) its tinted lid stands at its opening angle (default 70°) on two gas struts; unturned, the hinge is at the top of the floor plan. A datapoint can also give the angle (“Angle from a datapoint”, in ° or in %, then up to the opening angle): the lid stands open as far as it reports, with the angle shown beside it. Without a contact the hatch then counts as open from 1°. If it rains while the hatch is open, you get the same warning as for open windows.

Windows that open upwards: A top-hung window has its own opening angle and can also open inwards. The window style “Porthole / rounded” draws the frame and sash rounded – round when the window is as wide as it is high, otherwise with rounded ends – and opens upwards as a flap (default 60°). Tilted, both open at most about 26°.

Wind and weather

Under ⚙ → Weather & warnings you can choose sensors for wind (kn, km/h or m/s), wind direction (where it comes from, 0 = north), gusts and air pressure. Without them, or when a sensor reports nothing for an hour, the wind and air pressure of the weather entity apply. The Wind indicator (Furniture → Outside: windex at the masthead, windsock or flag – under “Which flag” e.g. Germany, Austria, Switzerland, France, Italy, Russia, Ukraine, other countries or the EU) turns with the wind and shows the wind, direction and Beaufort above it; gusts make it swing, and the windsock stands horizontal from 15 kn.

On the sea the waves run with the wind, with whitecaps from 4 Bft; gusts ruffle the water, and rain makes rings. In the natural styles (e.g. “Homely”) the deck and roofs get wet in the rain, and with sun and broken clouds, cloud shadows drift with the wind over the sea, the deck and the roofs. If the weather effect “Fog” is on, fog also comes from the visibility or from how close the temperature is to the dew point. If the air pressure falls by 3 hPa or more within 3 hours, a warning appears at the top (from 6 hPa a storm warning) and the sky gets darker.

Around the house

Views (Show): “Health” colours the rooms by their devices – red when one of them is unreachable, yellow for a low battery (20 % or less) – and shows badges at the devices and a list at the bottom right. With the mouse over a badge (or when you tap it), the tooltip tells which device it is, why, and when it last reported something. A device’s battery, LOWBAT, UNREACH and availability datapoints are found automatically next to its datapoint, as is whether its adapter is running and connected. Battery devices that sleep between two reports (e.g. Shelly H&T) only count as unreachable after 48 hours without a report. Under ⚙ → Weather & warnings you can set “Unreachable after … minutes without an update” instead (e.g. 15 for sensors that send every 5 minutes; contacts that only send on a change need more) and switch off that rooms without a problem appear (green, with “✓ n OK”) – then “Health” shows only problems. With “Always show battery and reachability problems at the device”, 🔋 and ⚠ also appear at the device outside “Health”, as well as at a hatch or a tank with its own sensor. Per device you can set this differently in the editor at the device (below its datapoint): as the device reports, after … minutes without an update, or never, plus “Check only while its adapter is connected” – e.g. for NMEA values whose instruments are switched off with the boat. This counts for the whole device, also for its other datapoints. Below that you see the battery that counts and where it comes from: found by the type-detector, guessed by name and role, or chosen here. If it is wrong, pick the right datapoint with “Choose …” (a number: charge in %, true/false: battery low) or “No battery” for devices on the on-board power supply; “Automatic” searches by itself again. “✓ 3 OK” at a room means: three devices there are fine; with the mouse over the room label you see which. A smoke, gas or water sensor warns for the room it is placed in: the room flashes red and the warning appears at the top. At the device in the editor you choose a symbol from the row under “Own symbol” (or type a Material Design name), and with “Colour when active” the colour it glows in while it is active. “Motion” colours the rooms by how often a motion sensor triggered there today (from the history).

Air the room: From a room’s temperature and humidity (and the outside temperature of the weather entity), the humidity in the coldest corner at the outer walls is estimated; from 70 % the corners turn dark, from 80 % (mould) “Air the room” appears. Switch it off under ⚙ → Weather & warnings → “Show the mould risk” (the air at open windows has its own switch). Open windows with a contact show the air: on the windward side it flows in, on the other side it flows out. If the room is heating (a thermostat in the room or a radiator with a thermostat), red warmth escapes, and after 10 minutes a warning appears.

Garden (Furniture → Outside): The Robot mower stands at its charging station; with its status (Worx code, Husqvarna activity or text) it drives lanes over the lawn while mowing and leaves stripes. The Sprinkler sprays while its valve is open; with a soil moisture sensor the lawn turns yellowish when it is dry.

Cars (Furniture → Vehicles): saloon, hatchback and van, in any colour you like. With an “At home” datapoint (tracker, sensor in the garage, on/off) the car reverses into its place when it arrives and drives away when it leaves; then its marking stays. Charging at a wallbox: when a wallbox with its status and power sensor hangs within 5 m of the car on the same floor, the car takes its cable as soon as the wallbox reports a car plugged in: it runs from the holster under the wallbox to the charging port above the wheel on the side facing the wallbox. The ring round the port glows in the colours of the wallbox card: green and pulsing while it charges (with lights running along the cable and ⚡ over the roof), green when it is full, amber while it is only plugged in, red on an error. With several cars near one wallbox, the nearest one hangs on its cable.

In the house (Furniture): spiral staircase (Stairs & railings; when placed it reaches up to the next floor and cuts a round opening there, “Mirror” turns it the other way), wood stove (Heating & climate; with its flue pipe up to the height set, none up to 1.2 m), oil tank (Heating & climate; the boat’s tank holding “heating oil”: the level in % and optionally the litres, yellow below 15 %), chimney (Architecture; with “Height above the floor” it can stand on the roof) and sauna (Bathroom; a cabin with a glass door, benches and the stove, open at the top). The corner bathtub (Bathroom) goes into the corner of the room with its back and left side (other corners: turn it); its front is cut at an angle, the tap stands on the deck in the corner, and with a datapoint for the water it runs in there. Also a railing (Stairs & railings; posts, bars and a handrail, e.g. along a gallery or a stairwell), outdoor steps (Garden & outdoors; solid steps of stone rising towards the back; their height is the difference in level) and the light well (Garden & outdoors; set it with its open back against the house wall in front of a cellar window, its height reaches from the floor up to the grate; it cuts its hole into the area outside around it, e.g. the lawn on a hill). Terrain: an area outside that lies above the ground with “Height offset” – a raised terrace, a level garden on a hill – is filled down to the ground; with “Slope” it becomes a bank. Under “Corners” every corner has a “Height” of its own above the area: in between, the area runs straight along its edges and gently curved inside – a hill rising to one side, a hollow, a bank in a corner. The plan shows the height beside the corner’s number. Where such an area lies clearly (more than half a metre) higher than the ground at the house, the floor stands in the earth there: it reaches up to the area, and windows below it get a light well – unless they have a “Light well” item of their own and “Light well” switched off at the window. Trees and bushes stand on the area they are planted on. Half floors (split level): each flight is a straight “Stairs” on the floor where it starts at the bottom, as high as the way up to the level it arrives at – from the garage to the ground floor, from there to the half floor and on to the hall. The flights are open below, so one can lie under another; each cuts an opening into the floor above it. The landing is a room of the half floor whose wall towards the flights has the height 0 under “Wall heights”; a “Floor opening” takes away the floor above the stairwell. Split level within a floor: if only part of a floor lies higher – the dining area three steps above the living room – it needs no floor of its own: enter “Floor raised (m)” at the room (up to 3 m). Its floor and the step come to that height, the room’s furniture, windows and doors count from its floor, and the walk goes up with it. The ceiling stays; for the same room height raise the “Ceiling height” by the same amount. The steps are a “Stairs” in the lower room. Wall: the area outside “Wall” is a stone block of its own height – a garden wall, or a retaining wall with the hill going on higher behind it. Cut into the roof: a roof terrace lying in the roof is a “Balcony” on the attic floor with the switch “Cut into the roof”. The roof is open above it; along its free edges walls stand from the deck up under the roof – the cheeks at the sides, the parapet in front. Where a wall is as high as a railing, the railing goes there. Draw the roof itself over it in one piece, without a gap. Gable windows up to the roof: for a window or door in a wall under the roof, the switch “Glazed up to the roof” puts a transom over it and fixed glass above up to the slope – into the peak when the ridge lies above it. The “Height” is that of the window with its sashes. Balcony railings stand only where it drops: not along the house wall, and not where an area outside – a garden on the hill, say – meets the deck at its level. Open space and gable windows: If a room is open up to the roof, draw a room “Open space” over it on the floor above and take its floor away with a “Floor opening”. Its walls reach up under the roof – gable and knee walls – and take windows; a window ends under the slope. Walls to the rooms beside it on that floor are inner walls, a window into it included.

Under ⚙ → Weather & warnings: “Severe weather warning” (level 1–4 or words like “severe”, e.g. from the DWD adapter) colours the frame and the banner. “Electricity price” (else the tariff from Energy) makes the house glow green when it is cheap compared with the rest of the day, red when it is dear; with a “Price forecast” (a JSON list with start time and price, e.g. from Tibber) the cheapest hour appears in the energy bar. “Water flow” (else the water meter from Energy): if water runs for more than 30 minutes, a warning appears. Shower, bath and washbasin show running water with their own sensor or valve or, if chosen, while the water meter counts. If a water detector reports a leak, the water rises in the room.

Speakers and TVs that are playing send out rings according to their volume, noise sensors (dB) from 50 dB.

Own 3D objects

If a piece of furniture is missing, use a model of your own: in the editor, place "Own 3D object" from Furniture (search "3D") and, under "3D model", upload a file or choose a model uploaded before. Best is GLB (glTF 2.0 in one file): it brings its colours and textures and is in metres. Blender, SketchUp, Fusion, Onshape and many model libraries export it. A glTF works only when its geometry and images are inside the file; the page cannot read models compressed with Draco or KTX2 textures – export without compression or with PNG/JPEG. OBJ and STL appear in one colour; they know no unit, so: larger than 8 means centimetres (OBJ) or millimetres (STL), larger than 500 means millimetres. A file may have at most 20 MB and 2 million triangles; above 300,000 triangles the 3D view can stutter on tablets.

The model stands with its underside on the floor, its front (−Y in Blender, +Z in glTF) faces down in the floor plan. Uploading sets width, depth and height to its original size; changing them stretches it, "Original size" sets them back. Rotate, mirror and "Height above floor" as with any furniture. One model can be used in several places and plans. Like any device it can be linked to a datapoint; a tap on it switches it. In the dark styles it gets glowing edges. Project export and backup take the models along; "Delete unused models" removes the files no plan (not even in the trash) uses any more.

Time travel

Time travel shows the house at an earlier point in time. The values come from the history adapters of ioBroker (history, influxdb, sql, recorder). Automatically it reads each datapoint from the instance that records it, otherwise from the default history of the system settings. In the editor under ⚙ → House → “Time travel reads from” you can prefer an instance, e.g. recorder.0: the recorder adapter chooses the important datapoints itself. Whatever the chosen instance does not record still comes from the history that records it. The parking spots follow the time too: a car stands there only if its sensor (“car present” at the parking spot or at the car) reported it then. When a car comes or leaves, “Parking spot taken” or “Parking spot free” is among the events (doors & windows); a tap on it flies to the spot. A history adapter has to record the sensor’s datapoint for this, otherwise the car is missing in the past.

Projects

As in vis-2, there can be several projects, say the house and the boat. Each has its own floor plans (with their bin), pictures, 3D objects, restore points, connected devices and settings (GPS position, time travel, "unreachable after"). In the editor, “⇅ Projects” manages them: open, create, rename, duplicate, delete and export as a file (download or save to home3d.0/exports/). Such a file becomes a new project when imported, and so does a “Backup” from the settings of the editor. Under “Floor plan files”, a single floor plan with its pictures and devices still moves into the open project.

A project opens by its link, index.html?project=boat; without project the default project opens. The link combines with the others, e.g. index.html?kiosk&project=boat for the wall tablet on the boat or index.html?view=card&project=boat in VIS. The header of the editor and the title of the tab show the name of the open project. The default project always stays, and the open project cannot be deleted.

Tablet and VIS

For a wall tablet: index.html?kiosk (“Kiosk ↗” in the header) shows the 3D view without the two bars at the top. The editor, “Projects” and “Connect devices” cannot be reached there. In the editor, “Kiosk ↗” opens the plan you are editing this way in a new tab. If the logged-in user may not write the files of home3d.0 (on a demo page, for example), the page only shows: as in the kiosk, without the editor, “Projects” and “Connect devices”; switching follows the rights of the datapoints. index.html?readonly forces this for users with write rights too.

To embed it as an iframe in VIS: /home3d/index.html?view=card on the same web instance as VIS shows only the 3D card, without the header, the floor bar and the editor; it fills the iframe.

Steering from ioBroker: writing a place or a device in words to home3d.0.show (e.g. "Lower deck", "Kitchen", "Diesel" or "house") makes every open 3D view fly there, but not one in the editor. So a voice assistant, pi-agent or a script shows on the wall tablet what is being talked about. The view confirms with ack and the name of what it shows. The names it knows are in home3d.0.places; with several projects, every open view looks in its own project, and places lists the other projects under projects. For pi-agent, home3d.0 needs a row with "Write allowed" in its write permissions.

Storage and permissions

Floor plans, restore points, images and device mappings are stored as files in the ioBroker file storage, each project in its folder home3d.0/<ID>/ (data.json, history/, images/, models/), the default project in home3d.0/default/. Exported files are in home3d.0/exports/. Login and permissions come from the web adapter: to save floor plans you need write permission on files; to switch devices, write permission on the datapoints.

Voice control and locating

Voice control works through the adapter Assistant: when an instance of assistant is switched on, the 3D view listens through the microphone, has the sentence recognized and answered there and speaks the answer. "Show …" (e.g. "Show me the kitchen") flies to a room or device. The browser allows the microphone only over https (or on localhost).

Locating (presence by Bluetooth) is not available in this ioBroker version: it would need a server that reads the receivers' signal strengths and learns the rooms. Links to it lead here.

Troubleshooting

“Offline” in the header: is the web instance running, and is its socket connection (integrated or via ws/socketio) active? Errors while saving: check the file permissions of the logged-in user. For device problems, check the object ID, the role and the write permission in ioBroker.
